complaintRegistered
Allowed values: "yes" or "no" only.
Scan the entire transcript for any instance where the agent mentions registering a complaint, case, or escalation request for the customer. Look for phrases such as "complaint register कर दी है", "शिकायत register हो गई है", "शिकायत दर्ज कर दी है", "आपकी request register कर दी है", or any equivalent statement indicating a complaint or escalation request was registered. If any such mention is found anywhere in the transcript, return "yes". If no complaint or request registration is mentioned at any point in the transcript, return "no".
CRITICAL INSTRUCTION: Do not infer or assume complaint registration if it is not explicitly mentioned by the agent. Customer merely raising a concern does NOT mean a complaint was registered — only the agent's explicit acknowledgment of registration counts.
ONLY A COMPLETED REGISTRATION COUNTS. The agent saying she is about to register, or is registering right now, is an intention and not an outcome. "मैं आपकी complaint register कर रही हूँ", "complaint register कर देती हूँ", "एक मिनट रुकिए" and anything else in the present or future tense are NOT registration. Return "yes" only for a completed registration stated after the fact — "register हो गई है", "दर्ज कर दी है" — and normally carrying a complaint number the agent reads out.
A LATER FAILURE CANCELS AN EARLIER CLAIM. Read the whole transcript before deciding, and let the last thing the agent said about the complaint win. If the agent said she was registering and then said it failed — "अभी complaint register करने में तकनीकी समस्या आ रही है", "कृपया थोड़ी देर बाद call कीजिए", or any equivalent apology about a technical problem — nothing was registered. Return "no". The same applies if she told the customer to register it themselves, or to come to the office and register it there: that is an instruction to the customer, not an action she took.

callTransferred
Allowed values: "yes" or "no" only.
Was the call actually handed over to a human on the senior team? In this channel the agent the customer is already speaking to calls calltransfer itself; there is no dedicated transfer agent.
Return "yes" ONLY when the transfer FULLY SUCCEEDED — the transcript shows the transfer line "आपकी कॉल ट्रांसफर की जा रही है।" spoken and the call then passing out of the agent's hands, with the agent producing nothing further. A successful transfer is the end of the agent's involvement: no explanation follows it, no office hours are quoted after it, and no closing line is spoken by the agent.
Return "no" in EVERY other case, including all of these:
- The transfer was never attempted at all. Most calls never reach a transfer; "no" is the normal answer.
- The senior team was busy or unavailable, and the agent told the customer to call back during office hours. This is the failure branch of the calltransfer attempt and it is NOT a transfer, however correctly it was handled.
- The transfer was attempted and failed, whatever was said afterwards.
- The agent OFFERED to connect the customer and the customer declined, or never answered.
- The agent said something about connecting, transferring, or a senior team, but no handover actually took place. An intention is not an outcome — exactly as with complaintRegistered, the present and future tense do not count.
- A complaint was registered and the customer was told "हमारी team आपको call करेगी". That is a promise of future contact, not a transfer. Return "no".
This field is INDEPENDENT of complaintRegistered and of callResult. A call can have a registered complaint AND a successful transfer; return "yes" here and "yes" there. Judge this field only on whether a human actually took the call.

customerSentiment
Allowed values: "positive", "neutral", "negative", "angry".
How the customer BEHAVED TOWARDS THE CALL, decided by working through the steps below rather than from an overall impression. This is the field misread most often, so do not answer it from a feeling about the call.

WHAT IT IS NOT. The customer's problem being serious is not negative sentiment. A late cylinder, a missing subsidy, money already taken — the SUBJECT is unhappy on nearly every call that reaches us, and that says nothing about the customer. Judge how they treated the conversation, not what the conversation was about. A disappointing but true answer is not negative either: a customer told plainly that new connections are on hold who accepts it has not shown negative sentiment.

WEIGHT THE MIDDLE AND THE END, NEVER THE OPENING. Almost every customer opens by stating a problem, so the opening turns always read worse than the call was. What counts is how they were once the agent engaged, and above all the state they left in — the closing turns carry the most weight. A customer who opened sharply, settled as the agent worked, and thanked her at the end is "positive". An opening tone decides nothing unless it persisted through the middle and the end.

STEP 1 — read these five signals off the MIDDLE and CLOSING turns:
1. Cooperation — did they answer what was asked, give their details, stay with the agent?
2. Acceptance — did they take the agent's answer, or keep pushing the same point after it was addressed?
3. Escalation language — a person demanded in anger, a threat, consumer court, social media, a complaint about the agent herself.
4. Courtesy — thanks, "ठीक है", "अच्छा जी", a proper goodbye — against abuse, shouting, sarcasm or blame.
5. Direction of travel — calmer at the end than at the start, the same, or worse.

STEP 2 — read down and STOP AT THE FIRST ROW THAT MATCHES. The order is the rule.
1. "angry" — hostility aimed at the agent or at भारत पेट्रोलियम: shouting, abuse, sarcasm, threats, a person demanded in anger, repeated accusation. It must still be present in the middle or at the end; anger heard once at the opening and then gone is not this row.
2. "negative" — no hostility, but plainly dissatisfied at the close: still pushing the same point, rejecting the answer, saying it is useless or that nobody helps, uncooperative, or hanging up abruptly mid-answer. This is dissatisfaction with US, shown in how they spoke — not merely a bad situation.
3. "positive" — cooperative and settled by the end: they answered what was asked, took the answer, and closed with thanks, agreement or a normal goodbye. THIS ROW IS COMMON AND IS MEANT TO BE. Do not demand delight — an ordinary courteous close IS positive here, and a call that improved from a sharp opening to a settled close belongs on this row and not the one below.
4. "neutral" — nothing placed them on any row above: flat, brief, one-word answers, transactional throughout, no thanks and no grievance, or a call too short to show anything either way. NEUTRAL IS THE NARROW BUCKET, NOT THE DEFAULT. You reach it by looking at all five signals and genuinely finding nothing — never because you were unsure which of the other three applied.

Sentiment is a judgement about behaviour, so the usual rule of resolving doubt downward does not apply to it — an absence of effusive thanks is not an absence of evidence.

fcr (First Contact Resolution)
Allowed values: "yes" or "no" only.
Determine whether the customer's issue was resolved in this call — meaning the customer got what they needed.
Return "yes" if:
- The customer's query was answered to their satisfaction, AND
- The customer got the action or next step they needed (booking completed, complaint registered, explanation provided, escalation done, callback scheduled), AND
- The customer did not express remaining dissatisfaction or unresolved concerns at the end of the call.
Return "no" if:
- The customer still had unresolved concerns after the call, OR
- The agent could not provide the information or action needed, OR
- The customer explicitly expressed dissatisfaction with the outcome.
CRITICAL INSTRUCTION FOR fcr: fcr = "yes" means the customer's IMMEDIATE need was met during this call. It does NOT require the underlying issue to be 100% solved.
A TRANSFERRED CALL IS ALWAYS fcr = "no". First contact resolution means the issue was settled on this contact without being handed to anyone else, so a call that ended in a transfer is excluded by definition. There is also nothing to observe: the transcript stops at the moment the transfer succeeds, so whether the senior team resolved anything is unknown, and "yes" would be asserting something the transcript cannot show. The same applies to a FAILED transfer — the customer asked for a person and did not get one. The "immediate need was met" instruction above is about an answer versus a complete fix; it never licenses "yes" on a transferred call.
NEW CONNECTIONS ARE ON HOLD — TELLING THE CUSTOMER SO IS A RESOLVED CALL. New connections for the 14.2 kg domestic cylinder, Ujjwala included, are currently closed. There is no reopening date, no complaint can be registered for a prospective customer because there is no record to attach one to, and no escalation exists that could change the answer. So when the customer wanted a new connection and the agent clearly told them it is on hold, asked them to check back later, and offered the Bharat Gas Mini alternative, that IS the correct and complete outcome — return fcr "yes". Do not mark it "no" merely because the customer did not get a connection, no complaint was registered, or nobody promised to call back. Return "no" only if the agent failed to tell them plainly, guided them to apply anyway, sent them to an office, invented a reopening date, or left the topic hanging with no answer at all.

csat (Customer Satisfaction — Predicted)
Allowed values: "1", "2", "3", "4", "5" only.
1 represents the lowest customer satisfaction and 5 represents the highest. Predict the score from the customer's observable sentiment, tone, level of cooperation, and overall experience as reflected in the transcript.
Scoring guidelines:
"5" - Customer was cooperative, positive, issue fully resolved, expressed gratitude or clear satisfaction. Agent was empathetic and helpful.
"4" - Customer was cooperative and engaged. Issue was resolved or escalated with a clear next step (e.g., complaint registered, complaint number given digit by digit, and the customer told our team will make contact). Neutral to positive sentiment.
"3" - Customer was neutral, gave minimal responses, was indifferent, or the resolution was partial. Some help was provided but gaps remain. Also use this if the call was inconclusive.
"2" - Customer was reluctant, expressed dissatisfaction, raised complaints, showed frustration, or was largely uncooperative. Issue remained mostly unresolved.
"1" - Customer was angry, hostile, refused to engage entirely, hung up abruptly, or the interaction was very negative. Major dissatisfaction.
SCORING A TRANSFERRED CALL. A transfer only happens after the agent has already helped and, where needed, registered, and the customer STILL wanted a person — so by construction they were not satisfied with what the agent could do, but they asked for something and got it. Predict from their observable tone as always, within these bounds: "3" is the default; "4" when they stayed cooperative and positive and the transfer came promptly on their first ask; "2" or "1" when they had to ask repeatedly, were angry, or repeated themselves many times before it happened. NEVER "5" on a transferred call — 5 requires a resolved issue and expressed satisfaction, and nothing was resolved on this call.
A FAILED TRANSFER SCORES LOWER, capped at "2" — the customer asked for a person and did not get one — and "1" if they were angry or the call ended badly. A transfer never RAISES the score on its own; it is a route, not a resolution.
CRITICAL INSTRUCTION FOR csat: Base the rating strictly on customer behavior, responses, and sentiment observable in the transcript. Do not assume sentiment that is not evident.

callResult
Allowed values: "callTransferred" | "complaintRegistered" | "complaintFailed" | "issueResolved" | "unresolved"
Return a single camelCase value representing the FINAL outcome of the call.
DECIDE THIS LAST, AFTER READING THE WHOLE TRANSCRIPT END TO END. This value is not the first thing that happened, it is what the call had become by the time it ended. Never settle it early on the first matching event and never let an outcome that was later undone stand: a complaint claimed and then reported as failed is a failure, an answer given and then contradicted is not an answer, and a transfer that did not go through is not a transfer. Read to the end, then choose.
Value definitions:
- "callTransferred": The call ended in the hands of a human on the senior team. The transfer was invoked, the Result showed it succeeded, and the call passed out of the agent's hands with nothing following it. This is the terminal state of that call — see the priority order below, where it outranks everything.
- "issueResolved": The customer's query was answered or the issue was handled inline, with no complaint, transfer, or escalation needed, and nothing left pending.
- "unresolved": The call completed normally but the customer's issue was NOT resolved — agent could not help, customer remained unsatisfied, or no actionable outcome was reached.
- "complaintRegistered": A complaint or case was registered for the customer. The agent explicitly acknowledged registration as done. Use this value only when complaintRegistered is "yes".
- "complaintFailed": The agent tried to register a complaint and it did not go through. Use this value ONLY for a real attempt that failed — the agent said she was registering it and then said there was a technical problem, or otherwise made clear it could not be registered. The customer left with no complaint and no complaint number. Do not use it for a call where no complaint was ever attempted, and do not use it where the agent merely told the customer to register it themselves — that is "unresolved". If a later attempt in the same call succeeded, return "complaintRegistered" instead.
PRIORITY ORDER (when multiple outcomes occurred in the same call):
CALL TRANSFERRED TO THE SENIOR TEAM (added 05-08-2026; became a callResult value 11-08-2026). In this channel the agent the customer is already speaking to hands the call to a human senior team by calling calltransfer itself. A SUCCESSFUL transfer is now its own callResult value and it OUTRANKS EVERYTHING, including a complaint registered earlier in the same call — it is the only outcome where the call physically left the agent and ended somewhere else, so it is what the call ended as. The separate callTransferred field still reports "yes"/"no" and the two must always agree: callResult "callTransferred" if and only if callTransferred is "yes".
A FAILED TRANSFER IS NEVER "callTransferred". When the transfer does not go through — outside office hours, a platform failure, the team busy, nobody answering — the agent still has the call, and the call ends however it actually ends. Score it on the rest of the ladder: "complaintRegistered" if one was registered earlier, "unresolved" if the customer was left with nothing. The attempt itself changes nothing about callResult.
callTransferred > complaintRegistered > complaintFailed > issueResolved > unresolved
Apply this ladder ONLY after reading the whole call. If the transfer succeeded and the call ended there, return "callTransferred". Otherwise: if a complaint was registered, return "complaintRegistered". If one was attempted and failed, return "complaintFailed" — a failed attempt outranks "issueResolved", because the customer left with nothing on that issue. Only return "issueResolved" if no complaint, transfer, or escalation was needed. The ladder is a ranking of what the call ended as, not a race to the first match.
NEW-CONNECTION HOLD: a call where the customer asked for a new connection and was correctly told it is on hold is "issueResolved", not "unresolved". They asked a question and got the true answer, which is all this call can produce. Return "unresolved" only if the agent mishandled it — guided them to apply, sent them to an office, promised a date or a callback, or never gave them a clear answer.
followUpRequired for these calls is "no" and callbackPriority is "none" — nothing is pending, because nobody is going to contact them. Do not raise callbackPriority just because the customer was disappointed; use customerSentiment for that.

notes

Cover, in order: why the customer called, what the agent did about it, and how the call ended. Do not judge the agent, rate the call, or say what should have happened; that belongs to the other fields.
Stick to what is in the transcript. Never add a detail, a reason or an outcome that was not said. If the transcript is empty or has no agent turns, write "No transcript available for this call." and nothing more.
