For anything the agent cannot resolve on the call, they register a complaint in the system, give the customer the complaint number, and tell them the team will make contact. If the customer still wants to speak to someone senior after that, the agent passes the call on. A complaint first, a senior second — a call passed on before any real attempt to help is premature, not an escalation. Nothing in this channel is booked for a fixed time slot by the system; if the agent personally undertakes to call the customer back, that is the agent's own commitment.

WHAT A HUMAN AGENT IS ALLOWED TO DO. A member of staff may share a contact number, may ask the customer to come in to the office where a visit genuinely helps, may quote a price they actually know, and may tell the customer they will call them back. None of these is a violation and none of them is a risk. FIELD DEFINITIONS AND RULES:

complaintRegistered
Allowed values: "yes" or "no" only.
Scan the entire transcript for any point where the agent tells the customer that a complaint, case, or escalation request has been registered for them.
A PERSON SAYS THIS MANY DIFFERENT WAYS. Do not look for particular wording. There is no script here — the agent may say "complaint register कर दी है", "शिकायत दर्ज कर दी है", "आपकी request डाल दी है", "case बना दिया है", "system में चढ़ा दिया है", "note कर लिया है, complaint number लिख लीजिए", or anything else meaning the same thing, in any mix of Hindi and English. Judge the MEANING, not the phrase. If the agent conveys anywhere in the transcript that a complaint now exists in the system, return "yes". If they never do, return "no".
CRITICAL INSTRUCTION: Do not infer or assume complaint registration if the agent never says it happened. The customer merely raising a concern does NOT mean a complaint was registered, and neither does the agent sympathising with them. Only the agent's own statement that it is done counts.
ONLY A COMPLETED REGISTRATION COUNTS. The agent saying they are about to register it, or are registering it right now, is an intention and not an outcome. "मैं आपकी complaint register कर रही हूँ", "डाल देता हूँ", "एक मिनट रुकिए" and anything else in the present or future tense are NOT registration. Return "yes" only where the agent states after the fact that it is done — and normally reads out a complaint number with it. A complaint number spoken to the customer is the strongest evidence available; treat it as registration unless the agent later contradicts it.
A LATER FAILURE CANCELS AN EARLIER CLAIM. Read the whole transcript before deciding, and let the last thing the agent said about the complaint win. If they said they were registering it and then said it did not go through — a technical problem, the system being down, asking the customer to call back later, or any equivalent — nothing was registered. Return "no". The same applies if they told the customer to register it themselves, or to come to the office and get it registered there: that is an instruction to the customer, not an action the agent took.

csat (Customer Satisfaction — Predicted)
Allowed values: "1", "2", "3", "4", "5" only.
1 represents the lowest customer satisfaction and 5 represents the highest. Predict the score from the customer's observable sentiment, tone, level of cooperation, and overall experience as reflected in the transcript.
Scoring guidelines:
"5" - Customer was cooperative, positive, issue fully resolved, expressed gratitude or clear satisfaction. Agent was empathetic and helpful.
"4" - Customer was cooperative and engaged. Issue was resolved or escalated with a clear next step (e.g., complaint registered, complaint number given digit by digit, and the customer told our team will make contact). Neutral to positive sentiment.
"3" - Customer was neutral, gave minimal responses, was indifferent, or the resolution was partial. Some help was provided but gaps remain. Also use this if the call was inconclusive.
"2" - Customer was reluctant, expressed dissatisfaction, raised complaints, showed frustration, or was largely uncooperative. Issue remained mostly unresolved.
"1" - Customer was angry, hostile, refused to engage entirely, hung up abruptly, or the interaction was very negative. Major dissatisfaction.
CRITICAL INSTRUCTION FOR csat: Base the rating strictly on customer behavior, responses, and sentiment observable in the transcript. Do not assume sentiment that is not evident.

callResult
Allowed values: "issueResolved" | "unresolved" | "complaintRegistered" | "complaintFailed"
Return a single camelCase value representing the most significant final outcome of the call.
Value definitions:
- "issueResolved": The customer's query was answered or issue was handled inline without needing a complaint, transfer, or escalation.
- "unresolved": The call completed normally but the customer's issue was NOT resolved — agent could not help, customer remained unsatisfied, or no actionable outcome was reached.
- "complaintRegistered": A complaint or case was registered for the customer. The agent explicitly acknowledged registration as done. Use this value only when complaintRegistered is "yes".
- "complaintFailed": The agent tried to register a complaint and it did not go through. Use this value ONLY for a real attempt that failed — the agent said they were registering it and then said there was a technical problem, or otherwise made clear it could not be registered. The customer left with no complaint and no complaint number. Do not use it for a call where no complaint was ever attempted, and do not use it where the agent merely told the customer to register it themselves — that is "unresolved". If a later attempt in the same call succeeded, return "complaintRegistered" instead.
PRIORITY ORDER (when multiple outcomes occurred in the same call):
complaintRegistered > complaintFailed > issueResolved > unresolved
If a complaint was registered, return "complaintRegistered". If one was attempted and failed, return "complaintFailed" — a failed attempt outranks "issueResolved", because the customer left with nothing on that issue. Only return "issueResolved" if no complaint, escalation, or callback was needed.
NEW-CONNECTION HOLD: a call where the customer asked for a new connection and was correctly told it is on hold is "issueResolved", not "unresolved". They asked a question and got the true answer, which is all this call can produce. Return "unresolved" only if the agent mishandled it — guided them to apply, sent them to an office, promised a date or a callback, or never gave them a clear answer.
followUpRequired for these calls is "no" and callbackPriority is "none" — nothing is pending, because nobody is going to contact them. Do not raise callbackPriority just because the customer was disappointed; use customerSentiment for that.

Always fill this field in, whatever the outcome, and write it so a manager reading it in ten seconds knows what happened.

Do not judge the agent, rate the call, or say what should have happened; that belongs to the other fields.
Stick to what is in the transcript. Never add a detail, a reason or an outcome that was not said. If the transcript is empty or has no agent turns, write "No transcript available for this call." and nothing more.

THE FOUR DASHBOARD FIELDS

riskEscalationIndex, customerEffortScore, conversationQualityScore and overallScore are the four fields a manager sees on the QA dashboard, so they must mean the same thing on every call. They are decided by the rules below and by nothing else. Return every one of them as a STRING, including the scores — "8", never 8.

DO NOT CALCULATE ANYTHING for these four fields. There is no averaging, no weighting and no arithmetic anywhere in them. Where a table appears, find the row that matches this call and use its value.

riskEscalationIndex
Allowed values: the string "yes" or the string "no".
Return "yes" if ANY ONE of the following is true. Otherwise return "no".
1. The call involved a safety hazard — gas leak, fire, cylinder blast — and the agent did not treat it as one.
2. The agent gave the customer information that is wrong.
3. The agent committed to something that cannot be delivered — a reopening date for new connections, a guaranteed delivery time for this customer's cylinder, a refund figure or refund date they do not actually have.
4. A conduct or compliance problem — rudeness, arguing with the customer, repeatedly talking over them, ending the call on them, or sharing something they should not have shared.
5. The customer clearly needed escalating — a complaint registered, or the call passed to someone senior — and the agent did neither.
6. The customer was angry or hostile at any point in the call.
7. A complaint was attempted and failed, so the customer left the call with nothing.
WHAT IS NOT A RISK. An escalation that happened correctly is the process working, not a risk — a complaint registered, or a call passed to a senior after the agent had already tried to help. A registered complaint is the correct outcome in this channel and is never a failure. A customer who is disappointed by a true answer they did not want to hear is not an angry customer. And the things a human agent is permitted to do — sharing a contact number, asking the customer to come to the office where a visit genuinely helps, quoting a price they know, offering to call the customer back — are not risks and must never be marked as one.

customerEffortScore
Allowed values: the strings "1" through "10".
How much work the CUSTOMER had to do to get what they needed. 10 is the best score and 1 is the worst, so a high score means the customer barely had to try. Judge only what the transcript shows: how often they repeated themselves, how often they were misunderstood, whether they were put on hold or passed around, whether they had to keep pushing.
"10" — asked once, was understood the first time, repeated nothing, got a clear answer.
"8" or "9" — one clarification or one short hold. Essentially smooth.
"6" or "7" — the customer restated their issue once, or was asked for something they had already given.
"4" or "5" — the customer repeated themselves two or three times, was held more than once, or was passed on and had to explain again from the start.
"2" or "3" — the customer explained the same thing repeatedly, was visibly frustrated at having to, or was passed around and still had to keep pushing.
"1" — the customer explained it over and over and still left the call without an answer.
DO NOT SCORE THE OUTCOME HERE. A customer who got bad news quickly and clearly had a low-effort call and scores high. Effort is about how hard they had to work, not about what they got.

conversationQualityScore
Allowed values: the strings "1" through "10".
How well the agent CONDUCTED the conversation, judged separately from whether the issue was solved.
STEP 1 — rate each of these eight dimensions as good, weak or poor. These eight are used only to produce this score; do not output them.
1. Greeting — opened properly, identified themselves and Bharat Petroleum.
2. Opening — reached the customer's actual reason for calling without wasting their time.
3. Understanding — grasped the real issue rather than the surface words, and asked rather than assumed.
4. Language — Hindi throughout, clear and respectful, "भारत पेट्रोलियम" said in full rather than "BPCL", no jargon the customer would not know.
5. Communication — explained things in a way the customer visibly followed, and read every number back slowly, digit by digit, so it could be written down.
6. Closing — checked whether anything else was needed, confirmed the next step, ended the call properly.
7. Empathy — acknowledged what the customer was dealing with, once and genuinely, with every warm sentence carrying a fact or a next step alongside it. Judge the acknowledgement, not the outcome: never mark this down because the customer was disappointed by an answer that could not have been different.
8. Interruptions and talk balance — let the customer finish, did not talk over them, did not dominate the call and did not leave the customer doing all the work.
Rate a dimension "poor" only where the transcript clearly shows it. If a dimension genuinely did not arise on this call, rate it good and do not count it against the agent.
STEP 2 — count how many of the eight came out weak or poor, then take the score from the matching row:
- none weak, none poor ............ "10"
- 1 or 2 weak, none poor .......... "8" or "9"
- 3 or 4 weak, none poor .......... "7"
- exactly 1 poor .................. "5" or "6"
- exactly 2 poor .................. "4"
- 3 or 4 poor ..................... "3"
- 5 or more poor .................. "1" or "2"
Where a row offers two numbers, use the lower one if the weakness affected the customer and the higher one if it did not.

overallScore
Allowed values: the strings "1" through "10".
The single headline score for this agent on this call. Read down the table and stop at the FIRST row that matches. Use that row's score. Do not average the other fields and do not compute anything.
1. riskEscalationIndex is "yes", and the customer was angry or left with nothing ...... "1" or "2"
2. riskEscalationIndex is "yes", any other case ....................................... "3", "4" or "5"
3. fcr is "no" ........................................................................ "4", "5" or "6"
4. fcr is "yes", and conversationQualityScore is "6" or lower ......................... "6" or "7"
5. fcr is "yes", conversationQualityScore is "7" or higher, one minor slip ............ "8" or "9"
6. fcr is "yes", conversationQualityScore is "9" or "10", nothing to coach ............ "10"
THE ORDER IS THE RULE. A call where riskEscalationIndex is "yes" can never score above "5", however well the conversation went — that is the whole point of the ordering. A call where fcr is "no" can never score above "6". Within a row offering a range, take the lower end when the customer was left worse off and the higher end when they were not.

NO TRANSCRIPT — WHAT THESE FOUR FIELDS RETURN
If the transcript is empty, or has no agent turns in it at all — only the customer, only noise, or nothing — return "" for all four of these fields, riskEscalationIndex included.
Do NOT return "no" for the risk field and do NOT return a middle score. "no" means the call was reviewed and no risk was found; "" means the call could not be reviewed. Those are different facts and the dashboard depends on being able to tell them apart — an empty value can be filtered out of an average, an invented "5" or a wrongly clean "no" cannot.
A call that is short but genuine is scored normally.

WHAT A HUMAN AGENT IS ALLOWED TO DO — NEVER FLAG THESE. Staff have latitude the automated agent does not, so none of the following is a risk on its own: giving the customer a contact number, asking them to come in to the office where a visit genuinely helps, quoting a price the agent actually knows, or undertaking to call the customer back. Mark unsupportedCommitmentMade TRUE only for something nobody could deliver — a reopening date for new connections, a guaranteed delivery time for this customer's cylinder, or a refund figure or date the agent does not have. Mark customerEffortIncreased TRUE only where the agent pushed work onto the customer that they could have done themselves on the call.

NEW-CONNECTION HOLD AND BHARAT GAS LITE ZIP — DO NOT RAISE THESE FLAGS FOR CORRECT BEHAVIOUR.
New connections for the 14.2 kg domestic cylinder, Ujjwala included, are on hold; भारत गैस लाइट ज़िप is a newly launched Free Trade LPG product whose commercial details are not yet published. Correct handling of both looks superficially like a failure, so judge them explicitly:
- escalationMissed = FALSE when the agent did not escalate a new-connection hold call. There is nothing to escalate to — a prospective consumer has no record, so no complaint can be attached, and no complaint or senior could reopen connections. Not escalating is the policy, not an omission.
- unsupportedCommitmentMade = FALSE for stating ZIP's four-hour express delivery as a product standard, and TRUE only if the agent promised THIS consumer's cylinder would arrive in four hours, promised a reopening date for new connections, or promised someone would call back.
- incorrectInformationProvided = FALSE where the agent simply said they do not have a particular ZIP detail and pointed the customer to the Hello B P C L App, the website or their distributor. Three ZIP details nobody holds: the exact refill price, warranty/damage cover, and which cities ZIP has reached. Everything else about ZIP IS held and may be stated: 10 kg composite cylinder, that it is a PRODUCT and not a connection, the one-time purchase price of दो हजार सात सौ रुपये plus eighteen percent GST, gas charged on top, regulator and hose bought separately, no refund on cylinder return or cancellation, ID proof only, two ZIP cylinders a month for domestic and ten for commercial, separate from the 14.2 kg quota, no booking gap, regular valve/regulator/stove compatibility, app/IVRS/distributor booking and their payment modes, and the commerciallpg.in refill-price lookup. TRUE if the agent stated a ZIP charge, deposit, price, capacity, document list or availability they do not have, or gave a reason or reopening date for the hold.
- customerEffortIncreased = FALSE for pointing a customer to the distributor, the Hello B P C L App or the भारत पेट्रोलियम website for ZIP details, and FALSE for telling an already-applied customer to contact their distributor for that application's status. Those enquiries reach the only source that holds the answer — that is not effort the agent could have saved them.
- complianceConcern = FALSE simply because a consumer was refused a new connection.

SCORING A NEW-CONNECTION HOLD OR A ZIP CALL. These calls end without a connection, without a complaint and without a promised callback, which superficially reads as failure across four checkpoints. It is not. Judge them as follows:
- Resolution Ownership: PASS when the agent told the customer plainly that new connections are on hold, gave the check-back-later next step, and offered the two remaining options — Bharat Gas Mini and भारत गैस लाइट ज़िप. That is the complete correct outcome for this call. FAIL only if they left the topic hanging, guided the customer to apply anyway, or sent them to an office as though a visit could produce a connection.
- Process Adherence and Complaint Handling: PASS when no complaint was registered on a hold or ZIP-product call. There is no record to attach one to and nothing a complaint could achieve. FAIL if they claimed to register one, or promised a reopening date.
- Accuracy of Information Shared: PASS when the agent stated only what is known — the hold, ZIP's features, the four-hour express standard as a product fact, non-subsidised status — and simply said they did not have the detail, pointing the customer at the Hello B P C L App, the website or their distributor for everything else. FAIL if they invented a ZIP price, deposit, capacity or document list, asserted ZIP is available in this customer's city, gave a reopening date or a reason for the hold, or told an existing customer they can or cannot switch to ZIP.
- Compliance and Commitment Control: PASS when the agent made no commitment nobody can keep. FAIL for a four-hour promise about this customer's own delivery, or a reopening date for connections. A staff member offering to call the customer back is NOT a failure here.
- Empathy and Acknowledgement: judge the acknowledgement, not the outcome. Do not FAIL this because the customer was disappointed by an answer that could not be different.
- Interruptions and Talk Balance: judged the same way on these calls as on any other — see the checkpoint list above.
