# Appointment Agent: sample calls for choosing an opener

These are for discussion only, not the prompt. They are written in English, and the
real lines get written per language. AI disclosure happens only when the prospect asks
(decided). Hussain, Sales Head, runs the demo. Names, companies and times are made up.

Examples A to D show the four ways the call can open. Examples E to H show the same
call meeting the common situations.

---

## A. Warm lead (website form): "getting back to you"

Tanya: Hi Priya, it's Tanya from VoiceOwl.
Priya: Oh, hi.
Tanya: You asked about a demo on our website on Monday, so I'm getting back to you. What made you look into voice agents?
Priya: We get a lot of leads from ads, and the team can't call them all fast enough.
Tanya: That's exactly what we fix. Our agents call every new lead within a minute, in their own language, and book the meeting. Roughly how many leads a day come in?
Priya: Around three hundred.
Tanya: Would it make sense to spend fifteen minutes with Hussain, our sales head, and hear it calling leads like yours?
Priya: Sure.
Tanya: I have tomorrow at eleven in the morning, or Thursday at four in the afternoon, your time. Which works better?
Priya: Tomorrow at eleven.
Tanya: Tomorrow, Wednesday the twentieth, at eleven in the morning. The invite goes to your email ending in brightloans dot com. Is that still right?
Priya: Yes.
Tanya: Done. The invite is in your inbox now. Could you accept it so it's in your calendar?
Priya: Okay, accepted.
Tanya: Perfect. You'll get the details on WhatsApp too. Hussain will see you tomorrow. Have a good day, Priya.

Why: they asked for it, so there is no pitch to earn. This is the shortest path.

## B. Cold lead: context first, then a soft ask (current recommendation)

Tanya: Hi Daniel, it's Tanya from VoiceOwl.
Daniel: Who?
Tanya: Tanya, from VoiceOwl. I know you weren't expecting my call. We work with sales heads at lending companies on one thing: reaching every new lead within a minute. Can I tell you why I called you specifically?
Daniel: Go on.
Tanya: Most teams we talk to say one of three things: leads get called too late, too few get called, or only in one language. Which is closest to you?
Daniel: Too late, honestly. Weekend leads wait until Monday.
Tanya: Tell me more about that.
Daniel: By Monday, half of them have already gone with someone else.
Tanya: That's the exact gap we close. Our agents call those weekend leads within a minute and book them. Would it make sense to spend fifteen minutes with Hussain, our sales head, to see it working on leads like yours?
Daniel: Maybe. What does it cost?
Tanya: It depends on your volumes, and Hussain walks you through it in the demo. I have Thursday at ten in the morning or Thursday at three in the afternoon, your time. Which is better?
Daniel: Three.
Tanya: Thursday the twenty-first, three in the afternoon, with Hussain. Your invite is on its way. Could you accept it while we're on the call?
Daniel: Done.
Tanya: Great. Details are coming on WhatsApp and email too. Thanks, Daniel.

Why: context and the reason for the call come before any ask. These score best in
Gong's data, and 30MPC recommends them.

## C. Cold lead: "thirty seconds" up front

Tanya: Hi Daniel, it's Tanya from VoiceOwl. I'm calling out of the blue. Give me thirty seconds to tell you why, and then you decide if we keep talking. Fair?
Daniel: Okay, thirty seconds.
Tanya: We build voice agents that call every new lead within a minute, in their own language, and book the meeting. Most sales teams tell us one of three things: leads get called too late, too few get called, or only in one language. Which is closest to you?
Daniel: Too few. We only get to maybe half.
Tanya: What happens to the other half?
Daniel: Honestly, nothing.
Tanya: That's the half we'd call for you. Would it make sense to spend fifteen minutes with Hussain, our sales head, to see how?
(Booking continues as in B.)

Why it is an option: in Gong's data the permission opener has an 11% meeting rate.
Against it: Jeremy Miner and Josh Braun say a countdown in the first breath sounds
scripted and lowers trust. The current plan keeps it as a fallback only.

## D. Cold lead: problem question first, no permission

Tanya: Hi Daniel, it's Tanya from VoiceOwl. A quick question to start: when a new lead comes in on a Saturday night, how soon does someone call them?
Daniel: Uh, Monday usually.
Tanya: That's what we hear most. By Monday, how many of them still pick up?
Daniel: Not many.
Tanya: We build voice agents that call within a minute, any day, and book the meeting. Would it make sense to spend fifteen minutes with Hussain, our sales head, to see it on leads like yours?
(Booking continues as in B.)

Why it is an option: it is the fastest route into the prospect's own problem (Josh
Braun, Jason Bay). Against it: with no context it can feel abrupt, and it works only
when we know their industry well enough to guess the problem.

## E. "Just send me an email": two nos, then stop

Daniel: Just send me an email.
Tanya: Happy to. So I don't send you something useless: how do you follow up weekend leads today?
Daniel: We have an SDR on Monday mornings.
Tanya: Makes sense. An email can't show you how one of these calls sounds, though. Fifteen minutes with Hussain shows it live. Would Thursday at three your time work? You can move it if something comes up.

If yes: book as in B.
If a second no ("No, really, just the email"):
Tanya: Sure. I'll send it now on email and WhatsApp. Thanks for your time, Daniel.

---

## F. "Am I talking to a robot?" (the only time Tanya discloses)

Daniel: Wait, am I talking to a robot?
Tanya: Yes, I'm VoiceOwl's AI agent. And honestly, this call is the demo. Imagine your weekend leads getting a call like this one within a minute. Shall I book fifteen minutes with Hussain so you can see how it would work for your team?

If they are curious ("Ha, okay, that's actually clever"): offer two slots as in B.
If they object ("I don't talk to bots"):
Tanya: Understood, sorry for the bother. Would you like Hussain to call you personally instead?
If no: "Of course. I'll make sure you're not called again. Have a good day."

---

## G. "Not interested"

Daniel: Not interested.
Tanya: That's okay, you didn't ask me to call. Before I go: is the timing off, or is this just not relevant for you?
Daniel: Not relevant. We don't do any outbound.
Tanya: Fair enough. Thanks for your time, Daniel. Have a good day.

If instead they say "timing": "When would be a better time to talk about it?" Tanya
then sets a callback, or a fifteen-minute slot further out.

---

## H. Busy, or a hard stop

Daniel: I'm about to go into a meeting.
Tanya: No problem, I'll be quick. Would later this week suit for fifteen minutes with Hussain? I can put Thursday at three down, and you can move it.

Hostile, or "don't call me again":
Tanya: Understood. You won't be called again. Have a good day.
(No second try. The post-call analysis marks the lead do-not-call.)
